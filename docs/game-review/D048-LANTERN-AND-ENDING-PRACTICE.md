## 4. Ask about meaning as well as listening to reading

Correct word reading alone does not establish comprehension or contextual vocabulary understanding. The agreed walkthrough adds one brief conversation after narration, using the actual ending and its source events. The child explains in their own words; exact speaking/typing controls remain for R13/R15/R16. A spoken answer needs its own visible listening state and should be evaluated for meaning separately from pronunciation. It is not a multiple-choice answer gate.

Agreed question:

> What does "kept his promise" mean here? What did Pip do that shows it?

Illustrative child answer:

> He said he'd plant the seed with Grandma before dark, and he did.

File-only positive acknowledgement, not separately shown in the accepted walkthrough:

> You connected his promise to what he did: planting the seed with Grandma before dark.

If the child only answers "He crossed the river", agreed follow-up:

> Crossing the river helped Pip reach Grandma. What had he promised to do with her?

Keep the relevant story source available for revisiting. A supported explanation can demonstrate the connection in this moment; a supplied hint, repeated model answer, or successful word match must not be recorded as independent mastery. Uncertain audio is not a comprehension failure. Accept equivalent explanations rather than matching one required sentence. Exact semantic interpretation, uncertainty, source references, typed/spoken controls and other questions remain for R11-R15/R19-R20 discussion.

This small ending example does not replace the reasoning embedded in the child's earlier story choices. ## Research informing the distinction

Checked September 13, 2026: the [IES/WWC grades 4-9 practice-guide summary](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/Docs/practiceguide/WWC-SummaryReadingInterven4-9.pdf) separately addresses word reading/fluency and comprehension, including answering and justifying text-related questions. This informs the proposed combined reading-and-meaning interaction; it does not validate the game's implementation or establish learning gains. [Proenca et al., Interspeech 2017](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2017/proenca17_interspeech.html) and [Gothi et al., Interspeech 2024](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2024/gothi24_interspeech.html) describe acoustic/phonetic methods and the difficulty of reliable child-reading error detection with limited false positives. Their results are not qualification of a provider for this demo. They inform the requirement to distinguish word recognition from supported pronunciation feedback.
