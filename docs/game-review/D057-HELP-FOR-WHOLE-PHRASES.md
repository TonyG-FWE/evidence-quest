## Kept his promise in the actual ending

Use the actual completed ending paragraph from [D042](D042-ENDING-NARRATION-AND-CHOICES.md), which is also used in D048's accepted feedback example:

> Pip crossed the river and planted the seed with Grandma. He had kept his promise.

If the child clicks **kept**, highlight **kept his promise** in the sentence and show this agreed help:

> **Kept his promise**
>
> Did what he had said he would do.
>
> Pip said he would plant the seed with Grandma before dark. He planted it with her.

The context explanation draws on Pip's actual opening promise and completed planting. It is not a new timer, alternate ending or claim that crossing alone fulfilled his promise. Do not replace the sentence's intended meaning with a generic kept definition about holding onto an object.

The child can hear the selected word **kept**, the phrase **kept his promise**, or the exact sentence **He had kept his promise.** Returning preserves the same paragraph, picture and position. Playback is a model, not an automatic microphone recording.

The existing D048 meaning question remains a separate agreed interaction. If the child first uses this help and then explains the phrase, the explanation has received support; it must not be recorded as an unaided response. No new meaning question or required answer is added here.

## Stayed put in the optional picnic story

In the full [The Windy Picnic page](D041-OLDER-LANTERN-STORIES.md), the actual sentence is:

If the child clicks **put**, highlight **stayed put** and show this agreed help:

They can hear **put**, **stayed put**, or the full actual sentence quoted above, then return to the same page and position. The help uses the existing paragraph about stones; it does not claim the wind stopped or create a new picnic task. The story remains optional.

## Coverage, control and evidence boundaries

The agreed pattern is that requested help identifies the word the child selected and explains the whole expression when that carries the sentence's meaning. It does not make every nearby group of words a phrase or silently select a different word for pronunciation. Whole-phrase highlighting and word/phrase/sentence playback must retain a clear connection to the actual source occurrence.

The six focus words remain unchanged, and support for other unfamiliar words remains available. Phrase help is optional and repeatable. It does not advance the story or establish vocabulary mastery.
