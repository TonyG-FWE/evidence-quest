> Rain dripped through the roof of Rina's bakery. She dragged her flour sacks away from the puddles.
>
> "I promised to have everyone's bread ready today," Rina said. "If those sacks get wet, I won't be able to bake."
>
> I found a cracked roof tile and replaced it. The dripping stopped.

>
> I packed my tools and went back to my workshop.

Below the passage, a separate empty writing area is labeled **Ending not written yet**. That label is interface text, not a sentence in Sol's story.

The repair took place before this conversation. Sol has not finished writing the account; the child is not being asked to perform the roof repair or invent a different past. Rina and her bakery are part of this remembered incident, not new present-day travel locations or inventory tasks.

## What the player can do

The passage stays available. The prompt is:

> How would you like to help Sol share his story?

Two equally presented choices appear, along with **Leave the conversation**.

### Choice A: help complete the account

Button:

> Let's finish the ending together.

Sol responds:

> "After I left, Rina baked the bread she'd promised. Later, she brought a loaf to the workshop to thank me. I could write about that."

Those two events are now available in Sol's own words. They remain visible beside the unfinished page. The game asks:

> Choose an ending using what Sol just told you.

The child sees two complete alternatives, with a preview of the corresponding moment.

**Ending A1 — Rina baking the bread**

> The repair had prevented the flour from getting wet. Rina used it to bake the bread she had promised. Fixing one tile had helped her keep that promise.

If selected, this paragraph fills the ending area. Sol says:

> "That explains why the repair mattered. I'll use that ending."

The preview shows Rina baking with the dry flour. It does not show the rain stopping or imply that damaged flour was restored.

**Ending A2 — Rina thanking Sol**

> Later, Rina brought me a loaf of bread to say thank you. I had thought it was only a small repair. It had mattered to her.

If selected, this paragraph fills the ending area. Sol says:

> "I hadn't thought to end with her visit. I'd like to tell it that way."

The preview shows Rina bringing the loaf to Sol. Sol's response explicitly accepts that way of telling his experience; the child does not silently rewrite his thoughts or the known events.

Both endings fit the page and Sol's additional account. The child can compare them, switch between them, return to the two main offers, or leave. There is no correct-ending score or compulsory mistake.

Sol's confirmation after either selection is:

> "I'll bring this version to the garden. I'd like people to hear what happened."

The page is now labeled **Ready to share**. Its author remains Sol; the game can acknowledge Pip's help selecting the ending without claiming that the child wrote every word. Selecting the ending does not establish oral reading or comprehension mastery.

## Specific help with the reading

The player can select this optional question beside the page:

> Did Sol make the rain stop?

Sol responds:

> "I stopped the water getting into the bakery. I didn't change the weather. Look at these sentences: 'I found a cracked roof tile and replaced it. The dripping stopped.'"

The cited sentences are highlighted. The original page, the choices, and the option to leave remain available. This explains the difference between repairing the leak and changing the weather without making an answer a progress requirement.

On ending A1, clicking **prevented** opens:

> **Prevented**
>
> Stopped something from happening.
>
> Replacing the tile stopped the flour from getting wet. The repair prevented that damage.

Controls: **Hear the word**, **Hear the sentence**, **Back to the story**. Word audio says **prevented**. Sentence audio says **The repair had prevented the flour from getting wet.** Help returns the child to the same paragraph and selection. This is an example of context-specific word help, not a restriction of support to this one word or one route. Vocabulary support and later reuse on the other route still require design.

**Hear the passage** is available for the displayed story and selected ending. **Practise a part** uses Rina's exact words:

> "I promised to have everyone's bread ready today. If those sacks get wet, I won't be able to bake."

The practice prompt is:

> Read Rina's words aloud. She is worried about losing the flour. Pause after each sentence.

The child can hear the exact passage, try it again, or return to the story. The practice excerpt removes the narrator's attribution and consists of Rina's two spoken sentences. It does not add new dialogue. Practice is optional; actual speech feedback remains open under R10/R13.
