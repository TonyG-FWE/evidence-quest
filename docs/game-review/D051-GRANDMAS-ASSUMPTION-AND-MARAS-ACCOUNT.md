## Agreed exchange before the automatic explanation

The child selects the existing **Share Mara's story with Grandma** and the actual page is handed over. Before Pip's automatic explanation, Grandma asks:

> What did Mara tell you about why she stopped visiting?

The child can answer in their own words. Example:

> She said she misses the garden. Her work hours changed, so she's still at the dock when the stories start.

An equivalent explanation is valid without quoting this exact wording. The comparison supports distinguishing Grandma's assumption from Mara's stated reason; it is not a test of whether the child memorized a scripted answer.

For this supported explanation, Pip supplies only the remaining page/request information:

> She gave me this copy for you. She'd like to hear your stories, too.

Grandma then uses her already-agreed D015/D025 response:

> I thought she'd stopped enjoying the garden. I should have asked her what had happened. Let's read what she sent.

This agreed change to the D025 report sequence lets the child explain the reason, uses the short Pip line to communicate the separate permission/request, and lets Grandma respond to the information actually received. It does not automatically supply the reason before asking the child about it.

## A misunderstanding and useful source-based help

Suppose the child says:

> She doesn't enjoy the stories anymore.

The game offers a source-based hint, shown as reading help rather than a statement Grandma somehow already knows:

> Mara said, "I miss it." What does that tell you about how she feels?

Keep Mara's full actual passage available, including the work-hours explanation, alongside Grandma's earlier account if requested. The child can revise their explanation or continue with help. Showing the child a source or hint is not automatically reporting that information to Grandma.

Offer the agreed **Let Pip explain** continuation, using the existing full D025 report:

> Mara still wants to come. She's working when the storytelling starts because she has to help the passengers off the last boat. She sent us her story, and she'd like to hear yours, too.

Then Grandma gives the same existing response and the story continues. This is explicitly supplied help, not evidence that the child independently explained the situation. The actual page handoff is not withheld while they answer. If interpretation is uncertain, retain the child's answer and the help/continuation route rather than treating them as wrong or silently rewriting their words.
