## Agreed focus words

The meanings below identify the intended sense in this story. They are not an assertion that these are each word's only meanings. The set and table's shown meanings are agreed. The source column retains the origin of the definitions, including mended's earlier draft and its new focused approval here.

| Focus word | Meaning in this story | Purpose and existing use/revisit opportunity | Source and current scope |
|---|---|---|---|
| secure | Hold something firmly in place. | Fasten the crossing; optionally explain how stones hold the picnic cloth down | D030/D037/D041; D055's application is agreed |
| obligation | Something you have a duty to do. | Understand Mara's responsibility to passengers and explain why she must finish work before leaving | D014 definition and duty account; D049 uses the idea without requiring this exact word |
| postpone / postponed | Move something to a later time. | Arrange a later gathering and communicate the changed time to Sol | D017 definition, D032 actual dialogue and notice; the past form's exact separate help remains to be specified |
| hesitated | Paused because you were unsure what to do. | Understand the boy's pause before accepting help; optionally encounter another boy pausing before playing the flute | D038/D054 exact context help; D052 inference stays separate from a stated motive |
| mended | Repaired something that was damaged. | Understand that Mara and the boy repair the sister's original paper bird, which remains the same gift | D038 contains the actual word; this definition from the earlier D015 draft is now agreed through D056; the full unshown help card remains separate |
| assumed | Believed something without checking that it was true. | Connect Grandma's earlier interpretation with the explanation she later hears and the account she writes | D040 exact definition and account; D051 practices the comparison before that later word encounter |

These are selected for their roles in actions, decisions and character accounts. Some may already be familiar to a particular child. No age-normalized difficulty rating or guarantee that each is a new word follows from this selection. Individual challenge/support and evidence of word learning remain R14/R20 work.

## Other words and phrases remain supported

The agreed six-word focus does not remove help for other unfamiliar words. Examples already in the material include prevented in the supplied bread ending, duet in an older story's title, and the phrase kept his promise in the final account. Their actual encounter conditions remain important: prevented is not guaranteed to appear in a child's own ending, and the older page is optional. D048 already covers the separate promise-meaning interaction.

Children can explain ideas naturally without using the target term in every answer. Do not require replacing a clear everyday expression with a focus word or mark a supported explanation wrong for using a synonym. Their actual wording, requested help and supplied text remain distinguishable.
