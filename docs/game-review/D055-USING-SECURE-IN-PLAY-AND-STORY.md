## Agreed optional connection in The Windy Picnic

This example occurs after the actual crossing interaction, if the child chooses to open the existing older story. Keep its full unchanged [page](D041-OLDER-LANTERN-STORIES.md) available. Its opening describes the wind catching a corner of the cloth; the relevant later excerpt is:

> "We'll need to secure the corners," he said.
>
> Mara fetched four smooth stones from beside the path. We placed one on each corner and set out our picnic.
>
> The wind ruffled our hair and rattled the leaves, but the cloth stayed put.

Offer an optional question in the reading interaction:

> What does secure mean in this story, and how did they do it?

Possible child explanation, not a required phrase:

> It means hold it in place. They put stones on the corners so the wind couldn't lift it.

Agreed game response:

> Here, stones hold the cloth in place. At the river, fastenings hold the crossing in place.

The meaning connects the two situations; the method is different. Securing the picnic cloth does not require a rope. Keep the picnic as a remembered account with its existing illustration, not a new object-manipulation task in the current garden.

## A misunderstanding and support

If the child says:

> Secure means stopping the wind.

Agreed response:

> The wind was still blowing. What stayed in place?

Retain the actual paragraph and D041's existing definition/context help, plus word/sentence pronunciation. The child can reread, explain again, request help or continue. Neither a mistake nor a retry is required, and supplied help is not independent meaning evidence.
