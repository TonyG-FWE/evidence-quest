## Starting point

Pip has Mara's page and her permission to share it, as established by D014 choice B. This scene begins once Pip reaches Grandma at the garden. The crossing and route between the dock and garden remain an explicit dependency; this scene does not transport Pip across the broken bridge automatically.

The player sees Grandma beside the garden's existing lantern-flowers. The current objective is:

> Share Mara's story with Grandma.

When the player clicks Grandma, the available action is **Share Mara's story**. Selecting it makes Pip hand her the page. This is the actual delivery event; possessing or viewing the page beforehand did not deliver it.

## Exact opening exchange

Pip says:

> "Mara still wants to come. But she's working at the dock while we're telling stories here. She asked me to share this with you."

Grandma responds:

> "I thought she'd stopped enjoying the garden. I should have asked her what had happened. Let's read what she sent."

Pip's words are the scripted explanation attached to the chosen delivery action. The game does not claim that the child independently supplied that explanation. Grandma receives the work-hours information through this exchange; she does not acquire it from an unvisited source.

## The actual story on Mara's page

The complete page appears, labeled **The Torn Wing — a story by Mara**. The first-person narrator is Mara; the boy's grandmother is a person within her account, not automatically Pip's Grandma.

> Everyone had stepped off the last boat except a boy holding a paper bird.
>

> "This is for my grandmother. My sister made it, but she's too ill to come. I promised my sister I'd keep it safe."
>

> "Would you like some help?" I asked.

>
> When he stepped onto the dock, he held the bird carefully in both hands.
>
> "Now I can tell my grandmother how we fixed it," he said.

The page has normal readable paragraphs and remains available throughout this interaction. The story is a short account of Mara's work, giving a concrete example of something she wanted to contribute. Its paper bird, boy, and repair belong to this remembered incident; they do not become new collectible objects or present-day characters.

## Reading and practice controls

The player can read the page silently or aloud. The controls are **Hear the story**, **Practise a part**, and **Continue**. Listening and practice do not advance the scene by themselves.

**Hear the story** reads exactly the displayed page and can be stopped or replayed. A player who wants to read aloud can keep the model stopped.

**Practise a part** shows the boy's exact words:

> "This is for my grandmother. My sister made it, but she's too ill to come. I promised my sister I'd keep it safe."

The instruction is:

> Read the boy's words aloud. He is worried about the damaged gift. Pause at each full stop, and let your voice show his worry.

The controls here are **Hear this part**, **Try it again**, and **Back to the story**. The text stays the same between attempts. The model uses the same words and pauses; the child can listen and reread. This is optional practice, with no microphone, speech score, or claim to have assessed the child's delivery. Actual oral-reading feedback remains open under R10/R13.

**Continue** proceeds to Grandma's response after the story is presented in the fictional scene. A button press records this scene transition, not evidence that the child read aloud, read accurately, or understood the passage. Leaving the conversation before finishing preserves the delivered page and allows the player to reopen it.

## Exact word help and a possible misunderstanding

Clicking **hesitated** opens:

> **Hesitated**
>
> Paused because you were unsure what to do.
>
> The boy does not hand over the bird immediately. He pauses before deciding to accept Mara's help.

Clicking **mended** opens:

> **Mended**
>
> Repaired something that was damaged.
>
> Mara and the boy repair the torn wing. They keep the bird his sister made.

Each card has **Hear the word**, **Hear the sentence**, and **Back to the story** controls. The corresponding sentence audio is respectively **He hesitated.** and **Together, we mended the wing.** These are examples within the broader D002 word-support requirement, not a decision to limit help to only these words.

An optional question remains available beside the page:

> Did Mara make him a new bird?

If the player selects it, Grandma responds:

> "She helped repair the bird his sister made. Look at this sentence: 'Together, we mended the wing.' Mended means repaired. He can still give his grandmother the gift from his sister."

The cited sentence is highlighted. The page and Continue remain available. This response explains the difference between repairing and replacing the gift; it does not require a correct answer before progress or infer that every player held this misunderstanding.

## Grandma's response and the new contribution

After the story, Grandma says:

> "I used to hear Mara's stories about the people she met at the dock. I've missed them. I'm glad you brought this one."

She looks toward a lantern-flower and says:

> "Let's keep her story here. You can choose which moment the lantern shows."

At this point, the story is added to one existing lantern-flower under the label **The Torn Wing — Mara**. Its default illustration shows Mara and the boy repairing the wing. Adding the contribution does not depend on answering the optional question, choosing a particular illustration, using word help, or performing aloud.

The child's choice of the displayed moment is agreed. Complete contribution and flower/image rules remain open. The proposed flower is already rooted in the garden: the scene neither grows a flower from an unplanted seed nor changes the existing seed/soil rule. One story contribution does not repair the bridge, change work hours, bring Mara to the garden, restore all lanterns, or finish the game.

## Player choice: which moment the lantern shows

Two illustrated choices appear with equal emphasis. The full story can be reopened while choosing.

### The boy holding the torn bird

Button:

> The boy holding the torn bird.

The lantern displays the boy holding the original bird carefully, with its damaged wing visible.

Grandma says:

> "He promised his sister he'd keep her gift safe. This moment shows why the torn wing matters to him."

The displayed caption is:

> A promise to his sister.

### Mara and the boy repairing the wing

Button:

> Mara and the boy repairing the wing.

The lantern displays Mara and the boy using tape to repair the original bird's wing.

Grandma says:

> "Mara asked before helping him. They repaired the bird together, so he could still give his grandmother the gift."

The displayed caption is:

> A gift repaired together.

Both choices depict events from the text and remain available. The player can switch between them or keep the default and continue. The title and full story remain attached to the same lantern; changing the chosen moment does not duplicate the contribution, change Mara's authorship, or rewrite the incident. This is a choice about presenting a story, not a claim that one interpretation is morally or educationally superior.
