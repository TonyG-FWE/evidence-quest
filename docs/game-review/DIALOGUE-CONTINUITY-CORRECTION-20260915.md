## What the child experiences

At the first dock conversation Mara explains that she must help the arriving passengers. The only story action is **Watch Mara help these passengers**. The passenger service happens in the world. Afterward, the conversation proceeds in three untimed parts: Pip asks about the story and Grandma’s assumption; Mara explains her changed work hours; Pip asks whether she can leave earlier and Mara explains her responsibility to the passengers. Each paragraph identifies its speaker or narrator. **Continue conversation** and **Choose Pip’s reply** lead through those parts.

Only then can Pip offer to read her page or ask Grandma about starting later. There is no offer claiming that bridge repair frees Mara from work. Making either offer does not commit the other, change the gathering time or send invitations. Rereading earlier parts does not undo an actual commitment. Help, word support, audio and practice stay available for the current passage; closing or reloading retains conversation progress. There is no reading countdown, required answer, word-exposure quota or claim that clicking Continue proves comprehension.

Pip can still cross, plant and visit the bakery before speaking to Mara. Grandma cannot plan around Mara’s work before Pip actually brings her account. After the account and report, Pip can ask for a later gathering before meeting Sol and without carrying Mara’s page. Giving the page, reporting Mara’s circumstances and actually telling The Torn Wing remain different events.
