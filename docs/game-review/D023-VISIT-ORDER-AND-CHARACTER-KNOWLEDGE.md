## Agreed approach

Give the child a clear suggested next step, while allowing exploration of places that Pip can physically reach. Conversation openings, available actions, and later reports follow what actually happened. Completed work remains completed, and unfinished work remains available when the child returns.

The suggested route helps the child understand the next useful action. It does not impose an arbitrary source-reading order or a quiz before movement. Physical conditions still apply: the river must be crossed through an available supported route. Planting with Grandma, delivering a page, planning an event, and actually attending it remain separate story actions.

## Worked example: Sol before Grandma

Starting conditions for this example: Pip has crossed using the joined boats and still carries the seed. He has not yet spoken to Grandma in person, and she has not asked him to invite Sol. The workshop is reachable along the garden-side path. Any actual prior Mara encounter/page remains preserved; it is not invented by this example.

The child chooses to visit Sol first. Use this opening, first sketched in D018:

> "Are you writing a story for the garden?" Pip asked.
>
> "Look at this one," Sol replied. "It's about fixing a baker's roof. I don't think anyone would want to hear it. I haven't even written an ending."

Sol slides his page toward Pip. The complete **A Small Repair — by Sol** passage and its two contribution paths are the same as D016. The new ordering changes the introduction and follow-up; it does not alter the historical roof repair or silently replace the story.

For this example the child chooses:

> **You could read what you have and ask people what they'd like to hear more about.**

The draft stays unfinished. The page remains Sol's, with the same **Draft to discuss** status. Because no gathering has yet been arranged with Grandma, Sol's closing line is:

> "I'd like to try that. Will you ask Grandma when we could share our stories?"

The suggested next step becomes **Tell Grandma about Sol's draft**. This is willingness to participate, not an already-issued invitation, fixed time, or arrival.

## Reporting the visit at the garden

At Grandma, the child selects **Tell Grandma about Sol**. Pip says:

> "I visited Sol's workshop. He'd like to bring his draft to the garden and ask what people want to hear more about."

Grandma replies:

> "I'd like that. Let's plant our seed together, and then we can arrange a gathering."

The report gives Grandma the information it actually contains: Sol wants to share a draft and invite discussion. It does not also tell her his private reason for previously staying away. D020's later direct question to Sol still has a purpose.

The immediate suggested task in this example is **Plant the seed with Grandma**. After planting, it stays planted. If Pip has Mara's page and has not shared it, **Share Mara's story with Grandma** is a remaining action. If Mara has not been asked, the child still has a reason to visit her. The planner can represent that missing information honestly rather than filling in her work hours or permission.

After the necessary actual conversations and reports for the chosen plan, the suggested next step is **Plan the gathering**. Grandma does not require another trip just to ask whether Sol has a story: he has already answered and she has received the report. A later trip can communicate the selected start time, as D018 already requires.

If Pip prepared an ending instead, the report must reflect that actual choice. Draft variant for later review:

> "I visited Sol's workshop. We worked on the ending of his story, and he'd like to share it at the garden."

That variant preserves the chosen ending and uses **Ready to share**. Its exact complete joined script is not approved merely by demonstrating the draft-discussion path.

## What visiting and returning do not decide by themselves

Distinguish what was presented to the player, what Pip learned within an actual scene, what another character has been told, and what physical action occurred. An available or held page is not automatically a read page, and a correct dialogue selection is not evidence that the child independently understood it.

A return visit can continue an unfinished conversation or communicate something new. It does not restart a completed planting, take another copy, discard Sol's selected mode, or silently change the gathering time. If the child leaves before completing a proposed task, retain the actual progress and next step; do not mark the full chapter complete from a plan or a title being opened.

Changing a plan after someone has been notified needs explicit treatment of the previous message and any new notification; [D024](D024-CHANGING-THE-GATHERING-PLAN.md) proposes that next. Replanning after the gathering begins, deliberately omitting a major story contribution, or finishing a shortened route remain unresolved; this agreement does not create new endings or hidden participation requirements for those cases.
