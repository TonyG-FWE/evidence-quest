## Purpose and scope of the example

Test a connected small mission: discover why Mara cannot currently join Grandma, help Pip cross and keep his planting promise, and bring Mara's actual page and explanation to Grandma. This combines a physical obstacle with a character problem that physical construction alone does not solve. The full agreed story still includes Sol, the gathering, Grandma's story, the selected closing, and the studio presentation.

The shown path uses D014's offer to share Mara's story and crosses while carrying the seed. It is one review walkthrough, not a replacement for the other agreed routes or the original five equally valid successful arrangements. The introductory crossing remains a modest problem; this encounter does not prove enough challenge for the entire chapter.

## What the child sees and controls

One coherent 3D view shows the starting bank, Mara's downstream passenger dock, the separate small garden boats upriver, the river, and Grandma's garden on the far bank. The fixed camera keeps the relevant action legible. Scene scale must consistently relate Pip, the boat tops, reachable paths, and the garden. Exact dimensions, art, camera framing, and movement distances are still design work, not supplied by arbitrary placeholders.

The child guides Pip around the walkable ground. Approaching Mara starts the conversation; approaching the small boats makes their description available. Reading appears in a clear panel over the same scene, with contextual word help. Closing it returns to the same world state.

Agreed construction interaction: the child briefly handles the movable paper-story pieces as the director while Pip waits on the bank. The child selects and positions the boats directly in the 3D scene, then returns to guiding Pip. This explicitly explains why a player can arrange pieces beyond Pip's reach; it is not an implied power that Pip secretly has. Tony accepted this hands-on construction choice in the shown encounter. It does not authorize manipulating arbitrary characters, carrying off scenery, or changing story facts.

Dragging is a candidate input, with a select-and-place equivalent so precision dragging is not mandatory. Pieces can align to supported connection positions. Arrangement, connection, and the later walk across must be visible actions, rather than one control performing the completed journey. Specific gestures, keyboard equivalents, alignment assistance, and camera transitions remain R15 decisions after the gameplay is reviewed.

## 1. A reason to investigate

The D018 opening is the reference:

> Pip had promised Grandma that they would plant a lantern seed together before dark. He put the seed in his backpack and set off for her garden.
>
> Last night's storm had washed away the footbridge. At the river, Pip stopped and took out Grandma's letter. She had written it before the storm.
>
> "Mara and Sol hardly visit anymore. I think they don't enjoy coming to the garden now. I miss hearing their stories."

Current goal: **Reach Grandma's garden and plant the seed together.** Near Mara, make **Talk to Mara about the garden** available. The child can inspect or approach the crossing first; the conversation is not a source-opening gate.

## 2. Mara's problem

Use D014's exact working exchange:

> Mara stood on the dock with a sheet of paper in her hand.
>
> "Is that a story?" Pip asked.
>
> "One I wrote for Grandma's garden," Mara replied.
>
> "Grandma thinks you don't enjoy going there anymore."
>
> Mara lowered the page. "I miss it. But my work hours changed before the storm. I used to finish before the storytelling began. Now I have to wait for the last boat to return. By then, everyone at the garden has gone home."
>
> "Could you leave a little earlier?"
>
> "I have an obligation to the passengers," Mara said. "I need to help them get safely off the boat."

Optional word help for **obligation**, from D014:

> Something you have a duty to do.
>
> Here, Mara means that helping the passengers get off the boat safely is her responsibility. She cannot leave before she has done that.

**Hear the word**, **Hear the sentence**, and **Back to the story** retain the same passage and world. Using help does not advance the boat schedule.

The shown path chooses the agreed response **I can read your story to Grandma.** Mara replies:

> "I'd like that. Take this copy with you. Tell Grandma I still want to hear her stories, too."

Mara visibly hands Pip the page. Pip now actually carries it; Grandma has not received or read it. Next goal: **Take Mara's story to Grandma.** The unfulfilled planting promise remains visible and is not replaced or completed by this new goal.

The other D014 response about arranging a later gathering remains a valid story direction. If the child assumes a crossing will let Mara attend at the usual time, use the established work-conflict response appropriate to the actual crossing state. On the initial broken-route state, the existing reply is:

> "Fixing the bridge would help me cross the river. But I would still be working when the storytelling starts."

The relevant work-hours passage stays available. No wrong-answer score or required mistaken choice is introduced. Crossing-aware variants must be written before a prototype offers that branch after construction; do not reuse a line implying the crossing is missing when it exists.

## 3. Build a route through the scene

Pip reaches the small garden boats. Use the D018 description:

> These small boats are kept here for anyone visiting the garden. One boat can carry the seed, but it cannot carry Pip.
>
> When the boats are joined, their wide tops make a footbridge. The joined boats are secured to both banks. Pip, Grandma, Mara, and Sol can walk across one at a time.
>
> The boats stay joined so everyone can return.

This is deliberately an introductory supported problem, not a claim that the text leaves a difficult hidden solution. The child must carry out construction and use the resulting route. Additional chapter challenges still need design.

In the agreed construction interaction, the child positions the paper boats in the scene and connects them. Exact attachment geometry remains open. The shown and accepted feedback for an arrangement that stops short of the far bank is:

> "This end doesn't reach the bank yet. Move the boats so the crossing reaches both sides."

The scene shows the actual gap. The pieces stay available for adjustment; the seed and page remain with Pip. The child can revise without restarting the conversation or losing possessions. No mistake is required.

When the supported arrangement is connected and secured, show the continuous crossing and its attachment to the banks. Return control to Pip. The child then guides Pip across the visible route, with the seed and page traveling with him. Walking across is a separate action from constructing the route. The crossing remains available for later return trips and guests.

Optional **secured** help, from D018:

> Fastened so something stays in place. Here, the joined boats are fastened to both sides of the river so the crossing stays in place.

The construction role, separate crossing action, visible gap feedback, and immediate revision are agreed. The number/shape of pieces, legal placements, fastening presentation, object reach rules, and full invalid-placement recovery still require a complete geometry/interaction contract before implementation. The text above does not silently approve a specific boat count, new rope/tool collection prerequisite, simulated instability, or arbitrary physics behavior.

The seed-first route remains valid in the story. Its exact boat travel/return and subsequent joining presentation must also be mapped before claiming the prototype supports it. This walkthrough's carrying route does not prove that mapping or justify eliminating the alternative.

## 4. Keep the planting promise

Pip reaches Grandma before any event-driven time advance. Grandma says, from D018:

> "You made it! Let's plant the seed while it's still light."

At the agreed planting place, the child starts **Plant the seed with Grandma**. Pip and Grandma visibly plant the actual carried seed. The world reflects its planted state; it is no longer in Pip's possession. If a supported seed-first route has been used, the seed must instead come from Grandma's actual possession.

This is a short story action, not an extra dexterity minigame. It does not instantly restore every lantern or complete the gathering. Reading/help never causes nightfall or a missed passenger boat.

## 5. A character consequence, not just a delivered item

The child chooses **Share Mara's story** beside Grandma. Pip visibly hands over the actual page and gives the D015 report:

> "Mara still wants to come. But she's working at the dock while we're telling stories here. She asked me to share this with you."

Grandma replies:

> "I thought she'd stopped enjoying the garden. I should have asked her what had happened. Let's read what she sent."

The page is now with Grandma and her knowledge changes through the actual exchange. Mara stays at work. Planting, crossing, page delivery, and communication of the reason are separately retained. The first encounter ends with Grandma opening **The Torn Wing** for the next agreed D015 scene. Delivery alone does not count as having read or shared the full story, create its illustrated lantern, or complete the chapter.

Proposed optional fluency activity: let the child practise Pip's exact report above and then read it during the exchange. Offer a model of that same text and an unhurried reread; continuing remains possible without speaking. This is purposeful practice, not assessed speech or proof of comprehension. The practice placement and exact controls are new D029 details, not already approved R10 behavior.
