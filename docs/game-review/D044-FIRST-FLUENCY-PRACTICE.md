## Model, child turn and purposeful rereading

1. **Hear this part** plays a natural spoken model of the exact displayed excerpt. Model the sentence boundaries and the boy's concern. Any highlighting follows the game's own audio, not the child's speech; do not imply microphone tracking. The child may stop/replay the model or read without listening first.
2. **My turn** stops model audio and keeps the excerpt visible while the child reads aloud. The first instruction remains available. Text and game progress wait for the child's next action; no audio completion, reading timer, detected speech or score is required.
3. **Try the last sentence** offers a different focus using the exact final sentence below. This option may be chosen without proving that the first reading happened. It is an authored practice cue, not feedback claiming to have detected an error.

> I promised my sister I'd keep it safe.

Agreed cue:

> Make promised stand out. The boy had said he would keep the bird safe.

Emphasize **promised** visually as the example focus, retaining the actual words and their order. The child can hear a model of this sentence with that emphasis, then read it again. This is one meaningful reading choice, not a test requiring a single correct emotional performance. Pronunciation/word meaning help remains available on the actual excerpt.

4. **Back to the story** returns to the same source page and ongoing story activity. The child may leave practice at any point. Repeated practice does not add lanterns, complete deliveries or overwrite a selected story. Exact control grouping, focus/scroll restoration and save behavior remain R15-R19.

## Game response and limits in this example

The game responds by playing the requested model, keeping the chosen passage available, offering the requested specific rereading cue and returning to play. It does not yet listen to or assess the child's voice. No statement such as You read that correctly or Your reading improved is generated by a button press. The model and cue are reading support; accurate oral-performance feedback is a separate design question still open in R10/R13/R20. This example neither selects a microphone implementation nor rules out a separately discussed optional feedback feature.

The existing independent-play and optional-practice direction remains. Listening without speaking, reading aloud, opening practice and actual story progression must not be conflated. The full behavior when model audio is unavailable, pronunciation help for a stumbling word, additional practice opportunities and passage/cue choices still need R10 review.

## Educational basis and evidence boundary

Read September 13, 2026: the US Institute of Education Sciences / What Works Clearinghouse [Providing Reading Interventions for Students in Grades 4-9, Recommendation 2](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/Docs/PracticeGuide/WWC-practice-guide-reading-intervention-full-text.pdf) supports purposeful repeated reading, work on expression, and varied reading opportunities. The [practice-guide summary](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/Docs/practiceguide/WWC-SummaryReadingInterven4-9.pdf) discusses short content-rich passages and how pauses/emphasis relate to meaning. This proposal applies those principles to a game interaction; the cited classroom/intervention evidence does not validate this self-directed demo, this exact excerpt, its dosage, or its effect on any child. The model/feedback difference remains material.
