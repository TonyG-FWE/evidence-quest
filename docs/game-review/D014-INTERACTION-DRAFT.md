## What happens in this interaction

The storm has damaged the bridge to Grandma's garden. Grandma believes Mara stopped visiting because she lost interest in the stories. The child directs Pip during a conversation with Mara at the dock to find out what happened and offer help. This conversation ends with a specific next action. The later visit to Grandma and the garden's restoration are separate events.

The proposed control is to click Mara to talk. The passage appears beside the scene and remains available while the child chooses a response. The relationship between these controls and the surrounding SparkFest experience remains an R04/R15 decision.

On-screen objective:

> Find out why Mara stopped visiting the garden.

## Exact passage

> Mara stood on the dock with a sheet of paper in her hand.
>
> "Is that a story?" Pip asked.
>
> "One I wrote for Grandma's garden," Mara replied.
>
> "Grandma thinks you don't enjoy going there anymore."
>
> Mara lowered the page. "I miss it. But my work hours changed before the storm. I used to finish before the storytelling began. Now I have to wait for the last boat to return. By then, everyone at the garden has gone home."
>
> "Could you leave a little earlier?"
>
> "I have an obligation to the passengers," Mara said. "I need to help them get safely off the boat."

The last two lines belong to the opening conversation. They are not attributed to an action the player has already chosen. The work change and storm order follow D012; the page, exact dialogue, and passenger duty are new proposed details.

## Optional word help

Selecting **obligation** opens this card without selecting a dialogue response or advancing the conversation:

> **Obligation**
>
> Something you have a duty to do.
>
> Here, Mara means that helping the passengers get off the boat safely is her responsibility. She cannot leave before she has done that.

Controls: **Hear the word**, **Hear the sentence**, **Back to the story**.

The word audio says "obligation." The sentence audio says "I have an obligation to the passengers." Closing the card returns to the same passage and choices. This specifies one word-help example, not the final vocabulary list or a restriction on support for other words. A lookup is not evidence that the child learned the word.

## Exact choices and responses

Prompt:

> What should Pip say?

The three response buttons appear together, with equal visual emphasis. The text above remains available.

### Choice A: ask for a different gathering time

Button:

> I'll ask Grandma to start the next gathering later.

Mara responds:

> "Please do. If it starts after the last boat returns, I can come once there's a crossing we can use."

Next action displayed:

> Ask Grandma about a later gathering.

This records an offer to ask Grandma. The gathering has not been rescheduled, the bridge has not been repaired, and Mara has not attended it. Those changes require their own later events.

### Choice B: offer to read Mara's story

Button:

> I can read your story to Grandma.

Mara responds:

> "I'd like that. Take this copy with you. Tell Grandma I still want to hear her stories, too."

Mara gives Pip the page she was reading. Pip holds it, and the item label is **Mara's story**. No borrowing condition or return task is added.

Next action displayed:

> Take Mara's story to Grandma.

This gives Pip the page and permission to share it. Neither Grandma's receipt of the page nor a reading of its contents has happened yet. The actual story on the page belongs to that later interaction and has not been authored here.

### Choice C: assume the bridge repair solves the whole problem

Button:

> I'll fix the bridge so you can come at the usual time.

Mara responds:

> "Fixing the bridge would help me cross the river. But I would still be working when the storytelling starts."

The passage highlights these exact sentences:

> I used to finish before the storytelling began. Now I have to wait for the last boat to return. By then, everyone at the garden has gone home.

Support text:

> Mara needs a way across the river and a way to take part while her work hours are different.

All three choices remain available, alongside **Leave the conversation**. The child can reread, select another offer, or leave and return. No correct-answer requirement, score, lost resource, or mandatory mistake is added. The game responds to the proposed action; it does not claim to know why the child chose it.

## Optional aloud reading at the end of the conversation

After an offer, show a short closing narration that matches what actually happened. It remains stable while the child listens or rereads.

Prompt:

> Read this part of the story aloud. Pause at the end of each sentence.

After choice A:

> Mara still wanted to visit the garden. Her work kept her at the dock until the storytelling was over. Pip offered to ask Grandma to start the next gathering later.

After choice B:

> Mara still wanted to visit the garden. Her work kept her at the dock until the storytelling was over. Pip offered to read her story to Grandma.

Controls: **Hear it read**, **Show pauses**, **Read it again**, **Continue**.

- **Hear it read** speaks exactly the displayed narration, with a pause at each full stop. It does not advance the game.
- **Show pauses** places each sentence on a separate line and shows **Pause** after each full stop. The pause labels are visual reading support, not added narration.
- **Read it again** keeps the same text and displays **Try the same passage again. Pause at each full stop.** It does not mark the child as more fluent.
- **Continue** closes the narration and returns to the scene with the next action for the chosen offer visible. Oral practice is optional; the child can continue immediately.

This proposal uses a model and an opportunity for purposeful rereading. It does not capture or assess the child's speech. Useful feedback on actual oral accuracy and expression remains an explicit R10/R13 decision. If both offers have been made on a return visit, preserve both facts; any later combined recap must reflect both rather than erase the earlier offer. Its wording would be authored before implementation.

## Why these actions relate to reading

The scene gives two reasons the child must keep separate: the storm damaged the crossing, and an earlier change in work hours prevented Mara from attending. Repairing the bridge addresses travel but leaves the work conflict. Asking for a later gathering or offering to share her story addresses that conflict in different ways. Mara's response to the bridge-only plan points to the relevant sentences and explains what that plan leaves unresolved.

The word-help card explains "obligation" using Mara's duty to her passengers. The closing passage gives the child specific text to hear and read aloud. These are designed practice opportunities; making an offer, opening a card, or clicking Continue does not establish comprehension, vocabulary mastery, or improved fluency.
