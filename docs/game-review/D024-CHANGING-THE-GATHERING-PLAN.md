## Question and agreed rule

Can the child change the time or storyteller after telling someone about the gathering, and how does everyone learn the new plan?

Agreed rule: allow changes before **Begin the gathering**. Show which people need new information, let Pip tell them through actual conversations, and preserve all other completed work. After the gathering starts, its confirmed time and invitations stay fixed for that event. The existing choices within storytelling, including helping Sol add an ending or keep his draft open, remain available.

Only confirmed use of a new plan changes the saved arrangement. Looking at another combination is a preview. Neither previewing nor saving broadcasts information to characters elsewhere.

## Worked example: usual time to later, with Mara reading

Starting conditions:

- Pip and Grandma have planted the seed. The crossing is usable in both directions.
- Mara's page and permission have been obtained, and her story has been shared with Grandma.
- The saved plan is **At the usual time / Pip reads**. Mara knows Pip will share her story while she works. Sol has been told the usual start time and has prepared **Draft to discuss**.
- The gathering has not started. Reading, help, and travel have not advanced the story to the event.

At Grandma, the child opens the plan and selects **After the last boat returns / Mara reads**. Grandma says:

> "We can wait until Mara finishes work. Tell Sol we're starting later, and ask Mara whether she'd like to read her story."

The child selects **Use this plan**, retaining the existing D017 action label. The current arrangement becomes the later gathering with Mara proposed as reader. Her acceptance is still pending. Two preparations are shown:

- **Tell Sol the new start time.**
- **Ask Mara to read after work.**

The planted flower, shared page, selected illustration, and Sol's draft stay as they were. Sol still expects the usual time until Pip speaks to him. Mara remains at work.

## Tell Sol what changed

Pip walks to the nearby workshop. The child selects **Tell Sol the new start time**.

> **Pip:** "We're starting after the last boat returns, so Mara can join us."
>
> **Sol:** "Thanks for letting me know. I'll bring my draft then."

This updates Sol's actual invitation. His draft stays unfinished and ready to discuss. The corresponding preparation is complete, while Mara's invitation remains outstanding. If the child returns to the planner now, it shows that actual difference rather than treating all invitations as done.

The finished-story path must retain its selected ending and use a contribution-appropriate response. Its exact line is an unshown variant, not approved through this draft example.

## Invite Mara at the dock

Pip uses the existing crossing to reach Mara. The child selects **Ask Mara to read after work**.

> **Pip:** "We're starting after the last boat returns. Would you like to come and read your story?"
>
> **Mara:** "Yes. I'll come over and read it once I've helped the passengers off."

Mara has now accepted the actual later invitation and reading role. She stays at the dock until her work ends. Pip returns to the garden; **Begin the gathering** can then advance story time through the agreed last-boat and guest-travel sequence, provided the other actual preparations are complete. Sol uses the workshop path, and Mara uses the established crossing after helping the passengers.

The gathering welcome, storyteller, and ending follow this updated arrangement. Any still-future task to take Grandma's story to an absent Mara changes with the plan; no delivered page or completed action is retroactively undone. In this demonstrated branch Mara attends and hears Grandma's story at the gathering.

## Beginning with an update still outstanding

If the child asks to begin while Sol still has the old time, Grandma says:

> "Sol is still expecting us at the usual time. Please tell him we're starting later."

Show **Tell Sol the new start time** as the next action and keep the plan available to revise. The event does not advance yet. This follows an actual invitation dependency, not a reading score, number of opened sources, or required explanation. There is no countdown while the child reads, uses word help, or makes the visit.

Likewise, proposed Mara participation stays pending until she is asked and answers. The full line for that outstanding invitation is a later exact-copy case. Readiness must consider actual current agreement and any previously invited person who needs a changed or withdrawn invitation; it cannot inspect only the new guest list and forget someone already told to come.

## Further continuity cases for discussion

| Situation | Proposed behavior |
|---|---|
| Preview a different plan, then close it without Use this plan | Retain the saved arrangement and all actual invitations |
| Save a different time before telling anyone | Update the pending invitations; no separate correction visit is needed for a message never delivered |
| Tell Sol the new time, then change back before telling Mara | Compare the current plan with each person's last actual conversation; Sol needs the changed time, and Mara needs only whatever differs from her actual information |
| Change a role without changing the time | Contact only the person whose accepted role or participation changes; Sol does not need a repeat visit for an unchanged time and contribution |
| Move from later/Mara to usual/Pip | Preserve Mara's work constraint and Pip's actual page permission; tell previously invited people about the change, including Mara, before beginning; the usual-time reciprocal story delivery is pending after the gathering |
| Leave or pause halfway through updating invitations | Retain the plan, each completed conversation, and the actual outstanding preparation; detailed save/input recovery remains R19 |
| Begin the confirmed gathering | Keep its time and invitations fixed for this event; retain D019's interactive story choices and optional reading support |
