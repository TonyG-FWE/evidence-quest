## Why this moment belongs in the adventure

At Sol's workbench, the child can already write an ending and use **Try my ending** to see its story scene. Offer the agreed separate **Read my ending aloud** action beside that scene-preview action. Reading lets the child hear how their wording will sound to the gathering and practise emphasizing why Sol's repair mattered. It does not accept the ending, perform it for the gathering or change the rehearsal's factual interpretation.

## Exact example and player action

Suppose the child has written D031's illustrative ending:

> I thought I had only fixed a roof tile. But keeping the flour dry helped Rina bake the bread she had promised.

This remains an example of child input, not a prefilled answer. **Read my ending aloud** opens the actual current ending exactly as written. The original story and Sol's disclosed later events remain available through the existing editor/source view.

Show this initial instruction:

> Read your ending aloud as Sol. Pause at each full stop.

**Hear this part** models the same words. **My turn** stops the model and leaves the words visible for independent reading. The separate **Read with feedback** option uses the agreed listening/Stop and D045-D046 outcomes only when explicitly requested and supported. Opening practice or choosing My turn does not start recording. A model or recognizer must use the actual current wording, not replace it with this sample.

## Purposeful rereading

Offer **Read it again** with this instruction:

> Choose the words that show why the repair mattered. Try reading those words with more emphasis.

In this example, the child might make **helped Rina bake the bread** stand out. This is one possible expressive choice, not a required span or scored answer. The cue asks them to connect what their ending means to how they read it. It does not claim the game heard insufficient expression or assessed comprehension. No new phrase-selection editor is implied.

If a word interrupts their reading, they can use its meaning/pronunciation help, then try the sentence containing it again. The support cue is:

> Listen to the word, then try it in the sentence.

Use that audio cue only where pronunciation audio is available. Otherwise retain the available text support and D046's audio fallback principle; exact word-help error labels remain R12/R15/R17. The child can return without listening or repeating.

## Return and actual story result

**Back to my ending** returns to the same writing/rehearsal view with their wording intact. They may keep it, revise it, or continue through the existing story options. Reading performance never gates an ending's use, NPC participation or progress. Accepting an ending and interpreting its factual content remain the distinct D031 writing/rehearsal flow; word-reading feedback is not approval of the draft's story facts.

If the child chooses one of the existing supplied endings, the same optional practice can use that selected text without labelling it child-authored. A child who keeps Sol's original unfinished draft can continue without inventing an ending to unlock practice.
